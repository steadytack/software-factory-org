# Got an idea for an app? Start here

Organisation A has a quick, safe way to turn an idea into a working app, built with AI and checked by people at every step. You don't need to be technical. You need a problem worth solving.

## What you can ask for
Anything that would save time, cut errors or make a job easier: a form that replaces a spreadsheet, a tool that matches invoices to orders, a board for swapping shifts, a dashboard of something you count by hand today. Small is good. Small ideas get built fastest.

## Two ways in
- **Develop it yourself** with the factory (`/factory` and your idea, in plain words). It works the idea through with you: asks what would make it fail, suggests other ways to get there, writes it up as a brief, assesses it and has it challenged. Go round as many times as you like until the idea is as strong as you can make it, then ask for sign-off. Ask the factory team to set you up.
- **Send an idea card** and the team develops it with you.

Either way, nothing goes further without **sign-off** from a named approver, who isn't you.

## How to send an idea card
Fill in the **idea card**: five short questions, in your own words.

1. **What's the problem?** What goes wrong or takes too long today?
2. **Who does it affect?** Which team or role, and roughly how many people?
3. **How is it done today?** The steps, the tools, where it waits or gets redone.
4. **What would better look like?** How you would know it worked: a number if you have one.
5. **What information does it use?** For example rotas, orders or customer records. Say where that information lives.

Don't worry about having all the answers. "I don't know" is a fine answer. The team will work out the rest with you.

**Describe information, never paste it.** Write "customer names and addresses from the CRM", not the names and addresses themselves. Never put personal details, customer records, prices, passwords or anything confidential in the card. The organisation's rules on information decide what the factory may use, and the team checks that for you.

### An example card
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What's the problem? | Swapping a shift means messaging the rota manager, who edits the spreadsheet by hand. Swaps get lost and shifts go uncovered. |
| Who does it affect? | Shop-floor staff at each store (about 40 per store) and their rota managers. |
| How is it done today? | Staff text the manager. The manager checks the rota spreadsheet, asks the other person, then edits it. |
| What would better look like? | Staff agree swaps between themselves, the manager approves with one click, and no shift goes uncovered. |
| What information does it use? | The rota, kept in a shared spreadsheet, and staff names from the HR system. |

## What happens next
| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Write it up** | Most ideas start as a **spike**: you (with the factory) or the team write a one-page **spike card**: the problem, the one question a small build would answer, how you would know it worked or should stop, and how long to spend. A bigger idea is written up in full first. |
| **2. Sign-off** | A named approver, not you, decides: **go**, **get ready** (fix something first, then look again) or **stop**. From here the factory team builds it, with you trying what they build. |
| **3. Try it** | The spike is built in a fixed time, usually a week or two, to answer its question. |
| **4. Grow it** | If the spike works, it can grow into a prototype people use, and later into a supported app. Each step up is a decision, never an accident. |

## What you get back
- **A reply to every idea card.** No idea disappears.
- **Your spike card, or a short overview of your idea:** the problem, what it would change, and how it was assessed.
- **The decision and why.** If it's "get ready", you hear what has to be fixed and when it will be looked at again. If it's "stop", you hear what would change the answer.

## The three sizes of app
- **Spike**: a quick try to answer one question. It may be thrown away.
- **Prototype**: people use it and react to it, to shape the idea. Kept simple underneath.
- **Production app**: relied on, fully tested, supported and looked after.

## Built the same way, every time
Every app the factory builds follows the same rules, the same safety checks and the same approved technology, whichever team it's for. So apps work in a familiar way, can be supported, and get better as the factory learns from each idea.

## It already knows a lot
The factory works from what the organisation already knows about itself: what each business does, every system we run and how they connect, and how our processes work. So you don't need to explain our systems. Say what's wrong, and the team will work out what already exists and what would change.

## What keeps it safe
- Nothing goes outside the organisation, or into a live system, without a person approving it.
- Only the kinds of information the organisation allows are used. Personal or confidential data needs extra approval, or made-up test data instead.
- Every app is checked against what was asked for before anyone uses it.
