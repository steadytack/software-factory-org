# The software factory: overview

For sponsors and leaders, in plain language: the sponsor overview every idea gets at its first decision, written for the factory itself. The detail is in [the brief](idea-brief.md) and [the assessment](assessment.md). Terms are in the [glossary](../glossary.md).

## Executive summary

**The problem.** Our staff have far more good ideas for better tools than IT can take on as projects, so most wait or are never raised. What does get built is built in many different ways, which makes it hard to support and secure. AI makes building faster, but on its own it makes that worse.

**The impact.** Every idea gets a fast, fair decision. Most are tried as a working version in about a week, and the ones that work grow into supported apps, all built one consistent way. We'll know it's working when ideas get a decision within two weeks, spikes finish within their time box, and published apps deliver the benefits they promised.

The reach and effort are early estimates; the pilot will replace them with real figures.

**The one thing to know or fix before investing.** No idea has been through the factory end to end yet. One real pilot will show how long it takes, how good the results are and how much effort the decisions need.

**Who benefits.**
- **Staff** get a way to have small ideas built, and a reply every time.
- **Teams** get tools that fit how they work and connect to the systems they already use.
- **IT** gets apps that are all built one way, on approved technology, which they can support, secure and retire.
- **Leaders** get every idea judged the same way, with evidence before money is spent and a review after.

**Effort and cost.** The factory itself is built. Opening it needs about 8 person-weeks *(assumed)*: naming the roles, bringing in the business context, the pilot, and the first months of operating. It runs on AI coding tools and hosting the organisation already approves. No new licence or service is needed.

**Key risks.**
- **The AI gets something wrong.** Independent challenge, automatic checks, audits and four human decisions are there to catch it.
- **The decisions become a bottleneck.** Named approvers, one-page decision summaries, and a limit on work in progress that grows with the team.
- **Prototypes quietly become relied on.** Moving up a size is always a decision, and only production apps go to a supporting team.

**The decision asked.** **Go as a spike:** run one small, real, low-risk idea through the factory to a working version, within two weeks. This commits one operator and one approver for those two weeks. It doesn't yet commit opening the factory to all staff; that is the next decision, made with the pilot's results.

## What it is
One production line for ideas. Anyone sends a five-question idea card. The factory drafts a short brief and an assessment, and a named person decides. Most ideas start as a spike: a working version in about a week, built to answer one question. A spike that works grows into a prototype people can try, then one that does real work, then a production app that is supported. Each step up is a decision.

Every idea, spec and app goes through the same line, so each one has:
- the same guardrails and approach;
- the same policies and defaults;
- the same knowledge of our business, systems and processes;
- technology picked from the same approved list.

All of that improves over time. What each idea teaches us becomes a proposed improvement, an approver decides, and the next idea starts from the better version.

## How it changes people's work
- **Staff with an idea:** today they ask a manager, raise a ticket or build a workaround. With the factory they fill in an idea card, get a decision within two weeks, and often see a working version a week later.
- **IT:** today each request is a case-by-case judgement and a project. With the factory, operators run the line, approvers make the decisions on one page each, and every app arrives built the same way.
- **Architecture and process owners:** today new tools appear without being added to their models. With the factory, every new app and its connections are offered back to them at release.
- **Leaders:** today ideas are hard to compare. With the factory, every idea is assessed the same way and ranked, with a review after release.

## In scope and out of scope
- **In scope first:** the pilot, with one idea using Internal data only, through to a working spike.
- **Next, if the pilot works:** naming the roles, bringing in the business context and the system landscape, and opening it to staff.
- **Out of scope for now:** connecting the architecture models directly, more technology templates, and ideas that need confidential or personal data before the controls for them are proven in a pilot.

## What happens next
On a Go as a spike, the pilot idea is chosen and run through the factory within two weeks. Its results come back to the sponsor for the next decision: open the factory to staff (and name the roles), run a second pilot, or stop.
