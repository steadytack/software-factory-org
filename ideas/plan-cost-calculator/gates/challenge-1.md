| 1 | plan.md, success criteria | The success test is circular. In session 2 the group tunes the weights while looking at the calculator, then the calculator is scored against the same five comparison sets those weights were tuned on. There are about ten free weights and only five outcomes, each just "the best plan in the set", so a match on 4 of 5 is close to guaranteed by fitting and says little about whether the model holds. The criterion needs held-out comparisons that the group ranks before it sees any calculator output, or rankings recorded blind in session 1 and scored against weights agreed in session 2. | Agreed. Session 1 now records the group's rankings of all eight sets blind, before any cost is shown; weights are set on sets 1-5 only, and success is scored on held-out sets 6-8 (at least 2 of 3), plus the implied trade-off ratios within the group's stated ranges (plan.md, Success and kill criteria). | changed |
| 2 | plan.md, kill criteria; assessment.md, problem | The main kill criterion ("two or more rules no £ term can reproduce") may never trigger. Any "never" can be written as a very large penalty, and the owner who built the tool also runs the sessions and decides whether a rule "fits". A sympathetic facilitator can always say a big enough weight or one more term covers it. The criterion should say who judges a rule as not reproducible, and that adding a new term or a prohibitive weight during the session counts as the model lacking that rule. | Agreed. The group names the rules itself, in its own words; a term or weight added in-session to cover a rule, or a penalty larger than the most expensive plan's total, counts as the model lacking it; finance confirms the match count (plan.md). | changed |
| 3 | idea-brief.md, assumptions; assessment.md, data and pre-mortem 2 | "Relative weights agreed on made-up rates carry over to real rates" is marked *(assumed)* but is probably false, not just unproven. If the made-up overtime, subcontractor, hotel and GSOS amounts sit in the wrong proportion to each other, the group sets the weights to make up for those wrong rates, and the agreed set will not hold once real rates go in. This weakens the "worth" (an agreed weight set to reuse). Data should be amber at best and arguably red for the stated purpose. Confidence should fall below 50 because of it, yet pre-mortem 2 says Confidence was "kept at 50" for it. Confidence lowered from 50 to 40 and the assumption marked doubtful; the spike now also records rate-independent trade-off ratios (hours of overtime per stay-away, per GSOS miss), stated by the group and implied by the weights. Data stays amber, not red: the question the spike answers (can the trade-offs be one £ model, and can people agree) does not need real rates, and nothing yet shows the data stops it working. | changed |
