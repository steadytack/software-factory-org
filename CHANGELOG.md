# Changelog

Changes to Organisation A's factory (stages, standards, scripts, schemas, the `factory` skill). Versions follow `factory/standards/versioning.md`.

## 0.14.0 (2026-11-01)
The leaders' guide said the factory doesn't replace projects for major systems, without saying what it does for them. A large specification (a scheduling and routing engine with a dozen integrations) showed the real limits: the approved technology, personal data and live systems of record.
- **Integrations by tier (DEF-24, new):** tiers 1 to 3 connect to stand-ins (a fake built from the other system's schema or a recorded exchange, filled with synthetic data, or that system's test environment); a live connection to a production system of record is tier 4. Each INT says which it uses, behind the adapter the live connection will use. In `tiers.md`, `spec.md` step 7 and `testing.md`.
- **Growing the approved technology, templates and components:** the guides describe the approved technology as templates plus a library of components joined with them and with each other. A "none fits" template at Spec, and a gap an approved component could have filled, is recorded as a DEC and raised with the factory owner as demand. Backfill comes first from what the organisation already runs: its stacks as templates, its libraries and code imported as components (`catalogue.py import --component`). In `spec.md` step 6, the operating model and the guides.
- **Publish offers reusable parts back** (`publish.md` step 8, new; the benefits review is now step 9): the app's reusable parts are listed in `decisions.md`, and each one approved at Gate 4 is imported to the catalogue and goes through its lifecycle.
- **Large programmes:** come in as several ideas, de-risked by spikes on synthetic data and stand-ins; the delivery runs as a project until the templates and data controls exist. The leaders' "What it isn't" is reworded to say so, and the technical leaders' known limits name it.
- **From SDLC to ADLC** (technical leaders' guide, new section): the factory mapped to the agentic development lifecycle, step by step and link by link of the artifact chain, with where it differs (a person at every gate, as proportional human review) and the gaps (continuous evals after release, production signals raising ideas), which become step 6 of the adoption path.
- **Glossary:** stand-in, synthetic data, ADLC, intent, artifact chain, executable gate, continuous evals, production signal, proportional human review.
- Migration: none. Existing ideas have no live integrations; an INT in a new spec pack states live or stand-in.

## 0.11.0 (2026-10-31)
The factory learned only from gate answers that differed from a recommendation. A clarification the owner gave anywhere else (a question round, mid-build, in conversation) stayed in that idea's `decisions.md`, and the next similar idea never saw it. And every idea read the whole of `learned.md`, another business's rows included.
- **Clarifications are recorded** (`learned.py clarify`, new): when the owner clarifies something at any stage, the factory records it in `decisions.md` as before and, if it could apply beyond this idea, appends it to `learned.md` with the key `clarify-<topic>` (Recommended is what the factory had assumed, or `none`), at the scope it judges, saying that scope in one line so the owner can correct it. The skill (step 6, new), `spec.md` (the question round), `context/README.md` and `learned.md` follow.
- **One is enough to propose a change:** `learned.py review` lists each clarification scoped beyond its project as a candidate on its own ("Write into factory/context/businesses/<b>/?"), since it is the owner's own statement, not an exception to wait for twice. Nothing is written into context or defaults by itself.
- **Recall by scope** (`learned.py recall <slug>` or `--scope S`, new): the rows an idea may consider, not superseded: its own project's, the global and app-type ones, its business's and the brands it may use. Never another business's or brand's. `scopes.py context` no longer lists `learned.md`; the skill runs `recall` at the start of every stage, and a recalled clarification is the starting assumption on its question, named where it is used (precedence 5).
- **App-type scope** `type:<kind>` (for example `type:field-app`) beside the bare `type`.
- Migration: none. `learned.md` has no rows and no idea exists.

## 0.10.0 (2026-10-31)
The rest of the review against recognised practice: what happens after release, architecture rules that are checked rather than read, threats and the supply chain, the move from the old way of working, and smaller registers and diagrams.
- **Benefits review after Publish** (`factory/project-files/benefits.md`, schema `benefits.schema.json`, new): `learned.py benefits <slug> [--published YYYY-MM-DD]` writes `benefits.md` at Publish, with the Gate 1 reach and impact and a 30-day and a 90-day review due from that date. Each review records the success measures (target, what was measured, met or not), the reach actually seen (0 is a real answer), whether the impact held, whether it is used as the to-be process expects, and lessons. `learned.py check <slug> --review 30|90` is its check. `ideas/pipeline.md` lists the reviews due ("Benefits reviews due") and flags one overdue; the status line counts them. `learned.py review` adds candidates: reach off by half or more (the median), impact that did not hold, or success measures missed in two or more published ideas, and the same lesson across outcomes and reviews. `publish.md` (step 7 and "The benefits review"), the skill, `context/README.md` and `factory/README.md` follow; `check_factory.py` validates every benefits file.
- **Architecture checks in the web-app template** (template 1.1.0, CAT-006 1.1.0): ruff now enforces complexity at most 12 (`C90`), at most 50 statements per function (`PLR0915`), no `global` (`PLW0603`) and no boolean positional parameters (`FBT001`, `FBT002`); `lint-imports` (import-linter, a new dev dependency) enforces `api` → `services` → `repositories` → `models` and keeps `core` and `schemas` out of the layers above (one named exception: the bootstrap admin's changelog entry); eslint's `no-restricted-imports` keeps `api`, `hooks` and `components` from importing the layers above them. Four boolean parameters became keyword-only, and two exceptions carry a `noqa` with the reason. `verify.py --template web-app` passed in clean containers (256 backend tests). React component length is not linted (116 functions are over 50 lines); the refactor audit looks at it. The stack standard has an "Architecture checks" section.
- **Threats, dependencies and the SBOM** (`security.md`): doc 07 gains a **Threats** table (Boundary, STRIDE, Threat, Mitigation); `check_spec.py` warns at protection B to D when there is none, when a category is outside STRIDE, or when no SEC, PERM, NFR or RISK in the pack answers a threat. **Vetting a new dependency** on its registry before it is added (it exists under that exact name, is maintained and widely used; AI tools invent names), recorded in `decisions.md` (`PRINCIPLES.md`, `build.md` step 8). An **SBOM** (CycloneDX) is published with the app at protection C and D: `publish.py` blocks without one; the web-app stack says how to generate it. **DPIA screening** before real personal data, a DPIA where the screening says so (UK GDPR article 35).
- **Transition** in doc 13: training, communication, cutover and support in the first weeks, each with who and when, or `Not applicable: reason`; `check_spec.py` warns when it is missing or neither a table nor not applicable. The brew-ratio fixture says not applicable.
- **Smaller:** a **work-in-progress limit** (DEF-23, new: at most 3 ideas past Gate 1 and not yet published), shown in `ideas/pipeline.md` with an "over the limit" flag and a note from `pipeline.py decide ... go` when a Go goes over; **C4 context and container views** in doc 10 and an **entity-relationship diagram** (`erDiagram`) in doc 06, as Mermaid, with warnings once there are two components or an integration, or two entities; **issues** (`ISS`, new ID prefix, an open item) in doc 09 for what has gone wrong, as opposed to a risk; **decision records**: a significant decision in `decisions.md` and doc 09 gives its context, the alternatives rejected and its consequences; **contract tests**: an INT no TEST names is a warning at tier 3 and above (`testing.md`).
- Migration: none. No idea exists in this repository; every new check on an existing pack is a warning, except the SBOM, which applies only when publishing at C or D.

## 0.9.0 (2026-10-31)
The factory drafted, rated and recommended its own work, and wrote the tests for its own code. Three changes give that work an independent check, and cover apps that call a model at run time.
- **Challenge before Gates 1 and 2** (`factory/standards/challenge.md`, new): a fresh context that did not draft the work (a subagent or a new session) argues against the ratings, the score and the recommendation at Gate 1, and against the pack at Gate 2 (untestable or unneeded requirements, missing negative cases, a heavier or lighter solution than the brief justifies, an unproven composition, a Gate 1 amber with no matching ASM, RISK or Q). It writes `gates/challenge-<gate>.md` (`| # | Where | Challenge | Answer | Outcome |`), and the factory answers each challenge as `changed` or `kept`. `check_spec.py` warns when the file is missing and blocks an unanswered row or a bad outcome, at Gate 1 (`--assessment`, not for a knocked-out idea) and Gate 2 (an idea in the factory). The gate page shows a Challenge line, and each kept challenge that disputes a rating, the score or the recommendation is a decision. `develop-assess.md`, `spec.md` (step 10) and the skill's gate protocol follow.
- **Pre-mortem in the assessment:** a `## Pre-mortem` section (the template has it) with the three likeliest reasons the idea fails, each naming its foundation and whether that rating allows for it, written before the one thing and the recommendation. `check_spec.py --assessment` blocks a missing or unwritten pre-mortem for an idea started on 0.9.0 or later and warns for an older one.
- **Tests come from the spec** (`testing.md`, `build.md` step 5, `PRINCIPLES.md`): a story's tests are written from its acceptance criteria and RULE examples before the code and seen to fail for the right reason; expected values come from the spec, never from the code's output; a test is never weakened to pass (a wrong AC is a change request); each test names the ID it proves. **Independent tests at the audit** (`audit.md` step 2, new; tiers 2 to 4): a fresh context that has not seen the build's tests or code writes tests from the pack against the app's outside, for every PERM, every RULE example and the negative and edge ACs of each Must FR; failures are findings, passes join the suite, and the verification report and Gate 3 give their counts. The audit's later steps are renumbered (`light.md` follows). The spec conformance audit also looks for tests whose expected values copy the code rather than the spec.
- **AI features** (`factory/standards/ai-features.md`, new; copied into each app as `standards/ai-features.md` by `scaffold.py app`): for an app that calls a model while it runs, what goes in the pack (docs 03, 05, 06, 07, 10, 11, 13), the rules (behind an adapter, the model ID pinned and prompts versioned, output treated as untrusted input, prompt injection, tools with the user's permissions and a person confirming irreversible actions, human review where the brief needs judgement, minimum data to the provider under DEF-11 and POL-11, timeouts, rate and cost limits, logging) and **evals** (a set built from the RULEs and ACs with hostile cases, sized by tier, graded by rules where possible, run against the real model before Gate 3 and after any model or prompt change, against a threshold in doc 05), with additions by protection level. `PRINCIPLES.md`, `security.md`, `testing.md`, `develop-assess.md` (the brief's Data and Risk), `spec.md`, `build.md`, `audit.md` and the standards index follow.
- Migration: none. No idea exists in this repository; an older idea is warned, not blocked, for a missing pre-mortem, and a missing challenge is a warning at any version.

## 0.6.0 (2026-10-31)
The tiers came from a single-person factory (Explore, Personal tool, Shared pilot, Operational) and mixed two things: how mature a build is and how much protection it needs. They are now separate.
- **Four tiers by maturity** (`factory/standards/tiers.md`, new): **1 Spike** (one question, fast, throwaway), **2 Exploration prototype** (people see it and react, to shape the idea), **3 Business prototype** (real users and real data; works end to end like a production system, simpler underneath, not production-grade testing or resilience), **4 Production app** (to be relied on). Real data starts at tier 3: tiers 1 and 2 use synthetic or public data only. Moving up is a gate decision, never drift: 1 and 2 grow at their Gate 3, and 3 becomes 4 by a change request that lists what tier 4 adds (`changes.md`, "Raising the tier").
- **Protection is separate from the tier** (`security.md`): levels A to D follow the data and who can reach it (A: builder or reviewers, synthetic data; B: organisation users, Internal data; C: Confidential or personal data, or many users; D: outside users, money or regulated decisions), at every tier.
- **The spike route becomes the light route** (`stages/light.md`, replacing `spike.md`; `plan.md` replacing the spike plan; `scaffold.py plan`; `pipeline.py light`), for tiers 1 and 2: `decide <slug> go --route light --tier 1|2` ("go as a spike", "go as a prototype"), time box 1 or 2 person-weeks by default. Gate 3 ends grow (a spike to tier 2 on the light route, or either to Spec at tier 3 or 4 with `--tier`), keep (as a demonstration on synthetic data) or stop. Schemas: the decision's `route` is `full` or `light` with `tier`; the state's `route` is `light` and `spike` becomes `light` (with `tier` and `to_tier`).
- **The full route is tier 3 or 4.** DEF-01 is now tier 3, Business prototype; `check_spec.py` warns when a definition in the factory is tier 1 or 2. The spec pack scales (`specification-pack.md`): tier 3 writes in full what real use needs and keeps 04 and 05 short; tier 4 writes everything.
- **Hosted in Docker at every tier** (DEF-02), developed and tested locally in the same containers; the host is for the organisation to name. DEF-03: a login for anything reachable by others, per-user accounts from tier 3.
- `PRINCIPLES.md` (copied into each app) loses its personal-project framing and its three hardening levels for the four tiers; `testing.md` sets test depth by tier; the hardening audit is required at tier 4; the operations and security lenses activate at tier 3; the definition template defaults to tier 3 and hosted; the skill, READMEs, stages and `factory/web/` follow. The `brew-ratio` fixture is now tier 3.
- Migration: none. No idea used the 0.5.0 spike route or a tier.

## 0.5.0 (2026-10-31)
A check that the factory develops ideas rather than blocking them found four places where it would hold a good idea back or slow it. Each is fixed.
- **An unknown is amber, not red.** 0.3.1 rated a foundation red when a screening question was unanswered, and red refuses a Go, so an idea was held back for being early. Now amber covers a question not yet answered (with what would settle it), and red is kept for a known blocker: data that exists but is not usable, no owner, a worst outcome that cannot be accepted (`develop-assess.md`).
- **Go as a spike** (`factory/stages/spike.md`, template `project-files/spike.md`, `scaffold.py spike`): when the value looks real and a small build would answer the ambers faster than more research. `spike.md` (the question, success and kill criteria decided up front, the time box, the smallest scope, the data and its classes, the template, throwaway or seed) is approved with Gate 1 in place of a spec pack and an overview; `pipeline.py decide <slug> go --route spike [--timebox N]` approves gates 1 and 2 and moves the idea to Build at tier 1. Gate 3 checks the result against the criteria and the outcome, then `pipeline.py spike <slug> grow|keep|stop`: grow goes to the full Spec (gates 2 and 3 reset, starting from what the spike showed), keep goes to Publish as a tier 1 tool (`publish.py` ships `spike.md` in place of the spec), stop closes it with its reason. A red foundation is still refused, and every policy applies in full.
- **Work it through.** Develop has an owner-led working session to thrash an idea out (alternatives, failure modes, a smaller first version) before or while the brief is drafted. DEF-17's limit is on the factory's questions, not the owner's thinking (DEF-17, the lenses' question rules and the skill say so).
- **Get Ready has an owner and a review date.** `decide ... get-ready` needs `--owner` and takes `--review` (four weeks by default; a past or malformed date is refused). `ideas/pipeline.md` has a Getting ready table (fix, owner, review date) and flags one past its date as overdue; the status line counts them ("past review"). At the review date the idea comes back to Gate 1, fixed or not.
- Schemas: the assessment's `decision` gains `route`, `timebox`, `owner` and `review`; the state gains `route`, `spike` (how it ended) and the Get Ready `owner` and `review`. `status.py` shows a spike's stage as "build (spike)". The skill, `factory/README.md`, `README.md`, `spec.md` (starting from a grown spike), `audit.md`, `publish.md`, the overview template and `factory/web/` follow.
- Migration: none. No idea is at Gate 1 or beyond, and every new field is optional for existing records.

## 0.4.0 (2026-10-31)

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-30)
A one-line idea cannot be assessed, so an idea is now developed before it is assessed, and assessed before anything is specified. The flow is Develop and Assess ─gate 1→ Spec ─gate 2→ Build and Audit ─gate 3→ Publish ─gate 4→ done.
- **Stage 1, Develop and Assess** (`factory/stages/develop-assess.md`) replaces Stage 0 Triage and Stage 1 Analyse (`triage.md` and `analyse.md` removed). Capture (inbox, a single idea or a supplied document; no filtering), then a 1-2 page **idea brief** per idea (`factory/project-files/idea-brief.md`: problem, outcome and success measures, today's process, data, risk, people and process, strategic fit, assumptions and unknowns; at most one question round), then an **assessment** (`factory/project-files/assessment.md`, schema `assessment.schema.json`): knock-outs, the **five foundations** (Problem, Value, Data Foundations, Risk, People & Process) each red, amber or green with a reason, RICE from the brief's evidence, and **the one thing** to know or fix before investing.
- **Gate 1 is the investment decision: Go, Get Ready or Stop.** Go moves the idea to Spec; Get Ready pauses it with the foundations to fix, then `pipeline.py reassess` opens a light re-assessment (the brief and assessment are kept; only the fixed foundations are re-rated, and the check refuses one whose reason did not change); Stop closes it with the reason. `overview.md` is now filled from the brief and the assessment (RICE, foundations, the one thing, the recommendation).
- `triage.py` becomes `pipeline.py` (`capture`, `add`, `develop`, `check`, `render`, `decide`, `reassess`), keeping the deterministic RICE scoring, ranking and POL-11 data-class checks; the register is `ideas/pipeline.json` (`IDEA-nnn`, schema `pipeline.schema.json`, replacing `triage.schema.json`) and the ranked view `ideas/pipeline.md`. Each idea gets its folder when it is developed, not when it is picked; Gate 0 is gone.
- **Spec** now opens with the solution choices that were in Analyse (tier, deployment, interfaces and design, template and runner-up, candidate assets, lenses) and the template table. `application-definition.md` is kept as a document, now written at the start of Spec and approved with the spec at Gate 2: it is the small machine-readable record the stubs, `check_spec.py` and the status page already read, so keeping it was simpler than folding it into docs 01, 02 and 10. Its problem, users and success sections moved to the brief.
- `check_spec.py --definition` becomes `--assessment`, the Gate 1 check: the brief (every section, no placeholder), the assessment (valid, no placeholder, five foundations rated, RICE, the one thing, a recommendation consistent with the ratings and knock-outs, re-assessments done) and the overview. The definition is checked with the pack at Gate 2.
- State: stage `analyse` becomes `develop`; gate `0` and the `triage` record are removed; gate 1 can be `get-ready` or `stopped`; an `assessment` summary and `versions.assessment` are added. `scaffold.py new` writes the brief and assessment stubs; `scaffold.py definition` no longer writes the overview. `status.py`, `dashboard.py`, `factory/web/`, the skill, the READMEs, the lenses, `changes.md` (which gate a change returns to), DEF-17 (one question round per stage), POL-11's wording, the versioning standard, the `brew-ratio` fixture and the tests follow.
- Migration: no ideas existed in this repository, so none needed migrating.
